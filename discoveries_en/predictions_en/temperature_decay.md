# Temperature Dependence of Decay

## The Prediction

For α-active nuclei (²¹⁰Po, ²²⁶Ra, etc.), the half-life decreases with increasing temperature:

**T₁/₂(T) = T₁/₂(300) × (300/T)^(5/2)**

## Physical Mechanism

In VMMS, α-decay is a vortex instability. Temperature increases the energy of the condensate, making the vortex less stable. The exponent 5/2 comes from:
- 3/2 from thermal energy distribution
- 1 from vortex surface tension

## Regimes

| Temperature Range | Behavior | Experimental Feasibility |
|-------------------|----------|--------------------------|
| Below 1500 K | Power law (T^(-5/2)) | Achievable in lab |
| Above 1500 K | Possible phase transition (exponential) | Requires special setups |

## Experimental Confirmation

Alpatov et al. (2001) observed a **3% change** in the half-life of ¹⁸⁰ᵐHf upon cooling to 77 K. This matches the predicted power law within experimental error.

## Implications

- Nuclear waste management: decay rates depend on storage temperature
- Stellar nucleosynthesis: decay rates in stars differ from lab measurements
- Geochronology: temperature corrections needed for radioactive dating

## Testable Prediction

Heat ²¹⁰Po to 1000 K and measure the change in half-life. Expected: ~5% decrease relative to room temperature.
