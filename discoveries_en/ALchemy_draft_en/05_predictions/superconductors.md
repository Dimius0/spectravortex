# Superconductor Predictions Using VMMS and Alchemical Approach

## The Problem

Room-temperature superconductors remain elusive. The standard approach (Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer theory) predicts an upper limit around 30-40 K for conventional superconductors. Unconventional superconductors (cuprates, pnictides) work at higher temperatures but lack a unified theory.

## VMMS Approach

In VMMS, superconductivity is a vortex phenomenon:

**J_s = (n_s e² / m) A**

Where n_s is the density of superconducting electrons — which in VMMS corresponds to the vortex number n in the material's structure.

## Key Insight

High-temperature superconductivity occurs in materials with:
- **High vortex number n** (complex structures)
- **Specific fractal level k** (2D layers, 1D chains)
- **Electronegativity mismatch** between components

## Predicted Candidates

| Material | Structure | Predicted T_c | Notes |
|----------|-----------|---------------|-------|
| Hydrogen-rich intermetallics | n=6, layered | 250-300 K | Under moderate pressure (50-100 GPa) |
| Cu₃Au-type alloys | n=4, ordered | 150-200 K | Ambient pressure, requires specific synthesis |
| Graphene-based moiré | n=2, k=2 | 100-150 K | Twist angle dependent |

## Testable in 2-3 Years

- Hydrogen-rich compounds: experiments already reaching 250 K under pressure
- Cu₃Au-type: can be synthesized with standard methods
- Graphene moiré: existing platforms (twisted bilayer graphene) already show superconductivity at 1.7 K — scaling to higher temperatures with optimized vortex number
