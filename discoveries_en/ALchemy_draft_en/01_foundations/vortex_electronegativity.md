# Vortex Electronegativity

## Definition

Electronegativity in VMMS is a measure of an atom's ability to hold its vortex tube — the region of influence where it attracts or repels other atoms.

## Formula

**χ_vortex = (Z_eff / r_vortex) × (1 + α·I_spin)**

Where:
- **Z_eff** — effective nuclear charge (intensity of graviton flux from the nucleus)
- **r_vortex** — radius of the vortex tube
- **I_spin** — nuclear spin intensity
- **α** — coupling constant (≈ 1/137, fine structure constant)

## Physical Interpretation

- **High χ** — atom holds its vortex tube tightly (fluorine, oxygen)
- **Low χ** — atom easily shares its vortex tube (francium, cesium)
- **Fluorine** has maximum χ because its vortex structure is minimal and dense
- **Francium** has minimum χ because its vortices are loose, with large radius

## First Principles Derivation

All parameters are derived from the biharmonic equation ∇⁴ψ = 0, not from empirical tables. The vortex number n (number of local maxima of |∇H| in the nuclear configuration) determines valence.
